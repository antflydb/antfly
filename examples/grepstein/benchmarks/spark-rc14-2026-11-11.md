# Grepstein on DGX Spark, 2026-11-11 UTC

These are short rc.14 stage measurements under **active GPU contention**, not a
completed corpus load, an accuracy qualification, an idle-device maximum, or an
A100 estimate. Another user's training job was left running. The benchmark's
owned inference/native processes exited afterward. Private source files, account
paths, process identities and document text are excluded from this report.

Runtime: `antfly 0.3.0-rc.14 (zig runtime)`. NVIDIA GB10, compute 12.1, driver 580.178.04.
Runtime SHA-256: `8e743bdab075dc6ef9696bb86e7fecdeafe51ec833fbca0d9c2458ddcfdf3468`.
Model IDs and individual observations are in the [measurement receipt](spark-rc14-2026-11-11.json).
They match the example's pinned LightOnOCR-3-4B, 768-dimensional EmbeddingGemma 2,
and GLiNER2.5 Transformers-wrapper revision.

The paired OCR run delivered 1.87x singleton throughput.
Cold calls in that campaign were approximately 47 s for OCR, 11.5 s for embedding,
and 5 s for extraction; they include loading/preparation and must not be confused
with resident throughput. The runner kept at most one model resident at a time.

A real dense page did **not** finish within a 55 s deadline with 8192 output tokens
allowed. The server returned `INFERENCE_TIMEOUT`; it contributes no completed-page
throughput. No estimate assumes it completed in 55 s.

## Corpus scale and limits

The existing sealed v1 manifest contains **4,422 records and
11,184 pages** across releases 1–7 and 12. A deterministic sample of one
PDF per release yielded 53 pages, of which 6
remain incomplete without OCR. This is not a population-representative sample:
release sizes and PDF lengths differ substantially.

If all 11,184 pages were OCR-required and as cheap as the tiny receipt,
the measured paired rate gives **18.3 hours** of OCR. Weighting each release by its
single sampled PDF gives about 7,896 OCR-required pages and **12.9 hours** at that
same short-page rate. Both are sensitivity scenarios, **not a full-example ETA**.
Dense pages can be much more expensive and have no completed timing in this run.
A mix with many dense scans can take days; this sample cannot bound that tail.

Native extraction was fast on the selected local PDFs, but the prepass omitted
GCS transport and did not claim complete index readiness while OCR debt remained.
Enrichment timings omit graph materialization/canonicalization, storage growth,
publication, retries, and query validation. Total chunk/window counts after real
OCR are also not known. Neither a 6/53 unweighted OCR fraction nor a fixed
chunks-per-page guess should be applied to the whole corpus.

To narrow the ETA, measure completed native extraction on a larger stratified
set, including dense pages, with the actual queue depth and a known idle/contended
GPU allocation. Capture OCR tokens, chunk/window counts, retry debt, index drain
and graph resolution. Scale measured successful work; retain failed samples as
debt instead of converting their deadlines into throughput.

## Reproduce

Use `benchmark_inference.py --ocr-concurrency 2` and `benchmark_corpus.py` from
this example with the released runtime and existing models. Keep input PDF copies
private and verify their generation-pinned SHA-256 before sampling. The runners
bind only loopback listeners, enforce explicit budgets and stop only their own
processes. No production Cloud tables or shared pool configuration were changed.
